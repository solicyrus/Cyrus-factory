Legal Requirements

⚖️ The law in British Columbia

Cooked meals (stews, curries, etc.) may not be sold from a home kitchen — not in person, not online, not through an app. Food for public sale must be prepared in a kitchen approved by Island Health (B.C. Food Premises Regulation).

Your 7-step checklist

1
Approved kitchen Cook in an Island Health-approved commercial or shared (commissary) kitchen. Home kitchens are not allowed for cooked meals.
2
Health operating permit Island Health food-premises approval, granted after an inspection of your kitchen. ≈ $150 / year
3
FOODSAFE Level 1 Food-handler certification, required for the operator. ≈ $99 in-person in Victoria · $25+ online · valid 5 years
4
Victoria business licence Required by the City of Victoria. Confirm your licence category when applying. ≈ $60 – $288 / year
5
Liability insurance Commercial general liability (≈ $2M) recommended. List Top 20 Chefs as additional insured.
6
Compliant labels Every dish needs: common name, ingredients (heaviest first), allergens in plain language, bilingual EN/FR, nutrition facts — including the mandatory front-of-package symbol (since Jan 2026) — plus maker name & address.
7
Tasting session In-person tasting with our team before you go live.

Not allowed 🚫

✕ Selling cooked meals made in a home kitchen
✕ Operating without a health permit, FOODSAFE, or business licence
✕ Skipping allergen or ingredient labelling

Penalties ⚠️

Violation tickets $115 – $345 per offence. Serious offences: up to $25,000 and/or 6 months jail. Health officers can close a kitchen and seize food on the spot.
